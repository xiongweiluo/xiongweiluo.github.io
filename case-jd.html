01Eight workspaces. Different ways to work.

Configure a fleet, trace a vehicle, resolve a repair—each workspace supports a different operational task.

02From a request to a deployment.

Operations teams track requests and apply for vehicles in one workspace. I connected the overview to grouped forms and region-dependent vehicle selection.

03Keep spatial work in context.

I grouped 47 map design nodes into 13 runtime states, keeping drawing tools, layers and version controls in the same workspace.

04Give each role the right access.

I preserved the permission hierarchy: choose a template, then edit its resources and actions in a dedicated drawer.

05The collaboration behind the product.

I designed the specialist roles, shared inputs and review handoffs. Agents work within defined scopes; I make trade-offs and accept the delivery.

06A prototype that travels.

The reviewed screens come together in a self-contained HTML prototype that can be opened offline.

English presentation reconstructions based on the original prototype; demo records and schematic geography. The vehicle artwork comes from the supplied prototype.

About this case

The diagram describes my project-level collaboration design. The examples come from the UI delivery branch; backend work is outside that branch, and individual agent execution logs are unavailable.

The 1 September 2026 audit covered 91 entries: 54 with source prototypes and 37 awaiting source material. The offline export was recorded on 7 September. Historical UI checks passed 317 tests; later reruns had intermittent timeouts. Production adoption and business impact were not measured.
